Problem 17 points
Ch. 5 — Total Series Resistance
Three resistors are connected end to end between terminals A and B: $R_1 = 560\ \Omega$, $R_2 = 1.2\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, and $R_3 = 3.3\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$. No other components are connected.
Three resistors in series between terminals A and B
Find the total resistance $R_T$ between terminals A and B, in ohms.[7 pts]
Solution
5060 Ω
Free-body diagram
Single current path through R1, R2 and R3
Step 1Sketch the circuit: Draw $R_1$, $R_2$, and $R_3$ end to end in one line between terminals A and B, and draw a single current arrow $I$ from A to B passing through all three.
Step 2List the knowns: $R_1 = 560\ \Omega$, $R_2 = 1.2\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, $R_3 = 3.3\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$. Asked for: the total resistance $R_T$ between A and B.
Step 3State the model: Tracing from A to B there is never a choice between two routes, so the three resistors are in series. Wire resistance is taken as zero.
Step 4Set the convention: Work in ohms throughout, using $1\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = 1000\ \Omega$.
Step 5Write the series rule: The same current must pass through every resistor in turn, so each adds its own opposition: $$R_T = R_1 + R_2 + R_3$$
Step 6Convert the units: $R_2 = 1.2\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = 1200\ \Omega$ and $R_3 = 3.3\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = 3300\ \Omega$.
Step 7Substitute the values: $$R_T = 560\ \Omega + 1200\ \Omega + 3300\ \Omega$$
Step 8Add the terms: $R_T = 5060\ \Omega$.
Step 9State the result: $R_T = 5060\ \Omega = 5.06\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$.
Step 10Check the answer: A series total must exceed the largest single resistor, and $5060\ \Omega > 3300\ \Omega$. All terms were in ohms, so the sum is in ohms.

Problem 27 points
Ch. 5 — Current in a Series Circuit
A $24\ \mathrm{V}$ DC source $V_S$ drives three resistors in series: $R_1 = 1.5\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, $R_2 = 2.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, and $R_3 = 4.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$.
A 24 V source driving three series resistors
Find the current $I$ in the circuit, in milliamperes.[7 pts]
Solution
2.70 mA
Free-body diagram
Series loop with the assumed clockwise current
Step 1Sketch the circuit: Redraw the loop with $V_S$ on the left and $R_1$, $R_2$, $R_3$ around it, and add one clockwise current arrow $I$ leaving the $+$ terminal of the source.
Step 2List the knowns: $V_S = 24\ \mathrm{V}$, $R_1 = 1.5\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, $R_2 = 2.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, $R_3 = 4.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$. Asked for: the circuit current $I$ in mA.
Step 3State the model: There is one path, so the current is identical at every point. The source and wires are ideal.
Step 4Set the convention: Conventional current flows clockwise, out of the $+$ terminal. Work in volts and kilohms, since $\mathrm{V}/\mathrm{k\Omega} = \mathrm{mA}$.
Step 5Write the series resistance rule: The resistors share one path, so $$R_T = R_1 + R_2 + R_3$$
Step 6Substitute into the resistance rule: $$R_T = 1.5\ \mathrm{k\Omega} + 2.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega} + 4.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = 8.9\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$$
Step 7Write Ohm's law for the whole circuit: The source voltage acts across the total resistance, so $$I = \frac{V_S}{R_T}$$
Step 8Substitute into Ohm's law: $$I = \frac{24\ \mathrm{V}}{8.9\ \mathrm{k\Omega}}$$
Step 9State the result: $I = 2.697\ \mathrm{mA} \approx 2.70\ \mathrm{mA}$, clockwise, the same through the source and all three resistors.
Step 10Check the answer: $2.697\ \mathrm{mA} \cdot 8.9\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = 24.0\ \mathrm{V}$, which returns the source voltage.

Problem 37 points
Ch. 5 — Unknown Resistor from a Measured Current
A $15\ \mathrm{V}$ DC source $V_S$ drives four resistors in series. Three are known: $R_1 = 2.2\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, $R_2 = 4.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, and $R_3 = 1.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$. The color bands on $R_4$ are unreadable. An ammeter inserted in the loop reads $I = 1.25\ \mathrm{mA}$.
Four-resistor series loop with an ammeter and one unknown resistor
Find the resistance of $R_4$, in kilohms.[7 pts]
Solution
4.10 kΩ
Free-body diagram
Series loop with the measured current direction
Step 1Sketch the circuit: Redraw the loop with $V_S$, $R_1$, $R_2$, $R_3$, and $R_4$ in one path, and mark the measured current $I = 1.25\ \mathrm{mA}$ clockwise out of the $+$ terminal.
Step 2List the knowns: $V_S = 15\ \mathrm{V}$, $I = 1.25\ \mathrm{mA}$, $R_1 = 2.2\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, $R_2 = 4.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, $R_3 = 1.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$. Asked for: $R_4$ in $\mathrm{k\Omega}$.
Step 3State the model: One path means the ammeter reading is the current in every resistor. The ammeter and wires add no resistance.
Step 4Set the convention: Current is clockwise. Work in volts, milliamperes, and kilohms, since $\mathrm{V}/\mathrm{mA} = \mathrm{k\Omega}$.
Step 5Write Ohm's law for the whole circuit: The source voltage and the loop current fix the total resistance: $$R_T = \frac{V_S}{I}$$
Step 6Substitute into Ohm's law: $$R_T = \frac{15\ \mathrm{V}}{1.25\ \mathrm{mA}} = 12.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$$
Step 7Write the series resistance rule solved for the unknown: Because $R_T = R_1 + R_2 + R_3 + R_4$, $$R_4 = R_T - (R_1 + R_2 + R_3)$$
Step 8Substitute the known resistances: $$R_4 = 12.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega} - (2.2 + 4.7 + 1.0)\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = 12.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega} - 7.9\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$$
Step 9State the result: $R_4 = 4.10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$.
Step 10Check the answer: With $R_T = 2.2 + 4.7 + 1.0 + 4.1 = 12.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, the current is $15\ \mathrm{V} / 12.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = 1.25\ \mathrm{mA}$, matching the ammeter.

Problem 47 points
Ch. 5 — Voltage Sources in Series
A battery holder connects four $1.5\ \mathrm{V}$ cells, B1 through B4, in series between output terminals X and Y. Cells B1, B2, and B4 are installed correctly, with their $+$ ends toward X. Cell B3 has been inserted backwards.
Four-cell holder with cell B3 reversed
Find the voltage $V_{XY}$ at the holder terminals.[7 pts]
Solution
3.00 V
Free-body diagram
Polarity arrows for each cell in the holder
Step 1Sketch the circuit: Draw the four cells in a row between X and Y. Mark the $+$ end of B1, B2, and B4 toward X and the $+$ end of B3 toward Y, and draw a polarity arrow over each cell.
Step 2List the knowns: Each cell is $1.5\ \mathrm{V}$; B1, B2, B4 point the same way; B3 is reversed. Asked for: the terminal voltage $V_{XY}$.
Step 3State the model: The cells are ideal sources in series with no load connected, so no current flows and no voltage is lost inside the cells.
Step 4Set the sign convention: A cell whose $+$ end faces X counts as positive; a cell whose $+$ end faces Y counts as negative.
Step 5Write the series-source rule: Sources in series add algebraically, respecting polarity: $$V_{XY} = V_{B1} + V_{B2} - V_{B3} + V_{B4}$$
Step 6Substitute the values: $$V_{XY} = 1.5\ \mathrm{V} + 1.5\ \mathrm{V} - 1.5\ \mathrm{V} + 1.5\ \mathrm{V}$$
Step 7Combine the terms: The reversed cell cancels one good cell, leaving $V_{XY} = 3.0\ \mathrm{V}$.
Step 8State the result: $V_{XY} = 3.00\ \mathrm{V}$, with X positive.
Step 9Check the answer: A correctly loaded holder gives $4 \cdot 1.5\ \mathrm{V} = 6.0\ \mathrm{V}$; one reversed cell in a four-cell holder leaves half of that, $3.0\ \mathrm{V}$.

Problem 57 points
Ch. 5 — Kirchhoff's Voltage Law
A $24\ \mathrm{V}$ DC source $V_S$ drives four resistors in series. A voltmeter gives these drops: $V_1 = 7.5\ \mathrm{V}$ across $R_1$, $V_2 = 4.8\ \mathrm{V}$ across $R_2$, and $V_4 = 6.2\ \mathrm{V}$ across $R_4$. Resistor $R_3$ is buried under a heat sink and cannot be probed, and no resistance values are known.
Four-resistor loop with three measured voltage drops
Find the voltage drop $V_3$ across $R_3$.[7 pts]
Solution
5.50 V
Free-body diagram
Loop with clockwise current and drop polarities for KVL
Step 1Sketch the circuit: Redraw the loop, add a clockwise current arrow $I$, and mark each resistor $+$ where the current enters and $-$ where it leaves.
Step 2List the knowns: $V_S = 24\ \mathrm{V}$, $V_1 = 7.5\ \mathrm{V}$, $V_2 = 4.8\ \mathrm{V}$, $V_4 = 6.2\ \mathrm{V}$. Asked for: $V_3$.
Step 3State the model: The whole circuit is one closed path, so Kirchhoff's voltage law applies to that loop. Wires drop no voltage.
Step 4Set the sign convention: Traveling clockwise, the source is a rise of $V_S$ and each resistor is a drop.
Step 5Write Kirchhoff's voltage law: The drops around the closed path must equal the source voltage: $$V_S = V_1 + V_2 + V_3 + V_4$$
Step 6Solve symbolically for the unknown: $$V_3 = V_S - (V_1 + V_2 + V_4)$$
Step 7Substitute the values: $$V_3 = 24\ \mathrm{V} - (7.5\ \mathrm{V} + 4.8\ \mathrm{V} + 6.2\ \mathrm{V}) = 24\ \mathrm{V} - 18.5\ \mathrm{V}$$
Step 8State the result: $V_3 = 5.50\ \mathrm{V}$, positive on the end of $R_3$ where the current enters.
Step 9Check the answer: $7.5 + 4.8 + 5.5 + 6.2 = 24.0\ \mathrm{V}$, the source voltage, so the loop closes. No resistance values were needed.
Worked Solution

Problem 67 points
Ch. 5 — Designing a Voltage Divider
A two-resistor voltage divider is connected across a $12\ \mathrm{V}$ DC source $V_S$. The lower resistor is $R_2 = 4.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, and the output $V_{out}$ is taken across $R_2$ with no load connected. The design requires $V_{out} = 5.0\ \mathrm{V}$.
Voltage divider with a required 5.0 V output
Find the value of the upper resistor $R_1$ that produces the required output, in kilohms.[7 pts]
Solution
6.58 kΩ
Free-body diagram
Divider with the common current and both drops marked
Step 1Sketch the circuit: Draw $V_S$ with $R_1$ stacked on $R_2$, mark one current $I$ downward through both, label the drop $V_1$ across $R_1$ and $V_{out} = 5.0\ \mathrm{V}$ across $R_2$.
Step 2List the knowns: $V_S = 12\ \mathrm{V}$, $R_2 = 4.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, required $V_{out} = 5.0\ \mathrm{V}$. Asked for: $R_1$ in $\mathrm{k\Omega}$.
Step 3State the model: The output is unloaded, so $R_1$ and $R_2$ carry the same current and form a plain series circuit.
Step 4Set the sign convention: Current flows down through $R_1$ then $R_2$; each drop is positive at its upper end.
Step 5Write Kirchhoff's voltage law for the loop: The two drops must add to the source voltage, so $$V_1 = V_S - V_{out}$$
Step 6Substitute into KVL: $$V_1 = 12\ \mathrm{V} - 5.0\ \mathrm{V} = 7.0\ \mathrm{V}$$
Step 7Write Ohm's law for $R_2$: The output voltage and $R_2$ set the common current: $$I = \frac{V_{out}}{R_2}$$
Step 8Substitute into Ohm's law for $R_2$: $$I = \frac{5.0\ \mathrm{V}}{4.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega}} = 1.064\ \mathrm{mA}$$
Step 9Write and evaluate Ohm's law for $R_1$: The same current flows through $R_1$ with $V_1$ across it: $$R_1 = \frac{V_1}{I} = \frac{7.0\ \mathrm{V}}{1.064\ \mathrm{mA}} = 6.58\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$$
Step 10State and check the result: $R_1 = 6.58\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$. Divider check: $12\ \mathrm{V} \cdot \dfrac{4.7}{6.58 + 4.7} = 5.00\ \mathrm{V}$, and $R_1 > R_2$ as expected because $R_1$ must take more than half of the source.

Problem 77 points
Ch. 5 — Voltage Divider Rule
A voltage divider made of $R_1 = 4.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ (upper) and $R_2 = 10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ (lower) is connected across a $9.0\ \mathrm{V}$ DC source $V_S$. The output is taken across $R_2$ with no load connected.
Two-resistor divider across a 9.0 V source
Find the output voltage $V_2$ across $R_2$.[7 pts]
Solution
6.12 V
Free-body diagram
Divider with the common current and the output drop marked
Step 1Sketch the circuit: Draw $V_S$ with $R_1$ above $R_2$, one downward current arrow $I$ through both, and mark $V_2$ across $R_2$ with $+$ at the top.
Step 2List the knowns: $V_S = 9.0\ \mathrm{V}$, $R_1 = 4.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, $R_2 = 10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$. Asked for: $V_2$.
Step 3State the model: With no load, $R_1$ and $R_2$ are in series and carry the same current.
Step 4Set the convention: $V_2$ is positive at the junction end of $R_2$. Both resistances are kept in kilohms so the ratio is unitless.
Step 5Write the voltage divider rule: A resistor takes the same share of the source voltage as it holds of the total resistance: $$V_2 = \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}\, V_S$$
Step 6Substitute the values: $$V_2 = \frac{10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}}{4.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega} + 10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}} \cdot 9.0\ \mathrm{V}$$
Step 7Simplify the ratio: $\dfrac{10}{14.7} = 0.6803$, so $V_2 = 0.6803 \cdot 9.0\ \mathrm{V}$.
Step 8State the result: $V_2 = 6.12\ \mathrm{V}$, positive at the junction of $R_1$ and $R_2$.
Step 9Check the answer: $R_2$ is about $68\%$ of the total resistance, so it should take about $68\%$ of $9.0\ \mathrm{V}$; the remaining $2.88\ \mathrm{V}$ across $R_1$ brings the sum back to $9.0\ \mathrm{V}$.

Problem 87 points
Ch. 5 — Potentiometer as a Variable Divider
A fixed resistor $R_1 = 1.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ is in series with the full track of a potentiometer $R_P = 5.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ across a $12\ \mathrm{V}$ DC source $V_S$. The bottom end of the track is connected to the negative terminal, which is ground. The wiper W is set $40\%$ of the way up the track from the bottom end, and the output $V_{out}$ is taken from the wiper to ground with no load connected.
Fixed resistor in series with a potentiometer used as a divider
Find the output voltage $V_{out}$.[7 pts]
Solution
4.00 V
Free-body diagram
Track split into upper and lower sections with the common current
Step 1Sketch the circuit: Redraw the potentiometer as two resistors, $R_{upper}$ above the wiper and $R_{lower}$ below it, in series with $R_1$, and mark one downward current $I$ through all three.
Step 2List the knowns: $V_S = 12\ \mathrm{V}$, $R_1 = 1.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, $R_P = 5.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, wiper at $40\%$ from the bottom. Asked for: $V_{out}$ from wiper to ground.
Step 3State the model: The track resistance is uniform, and the unloaded wiper draws no current, so $R_1$, $R_{upper}$, and $R_{lower}$ are in series.
Step 4Set the convention: Ground is $0\ \mathrm{V}$ at the bottom of the track; $V_{out}$ is the drop across $R_{lower}$, positive at the wiper.
Step 5Write the track-split relation: The section below the wiper is the stated fraction of the track: $$R_{lower} = 0.40\, R_P$$
Step 6Substitute into the track-split relation: $$R_{lower} = 0.40 \cdot 5.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = 2.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$$
Step 7Write the series resistance rule: The whole track stays in the current path wherever the wiper sits, so $$R_T = R_1 + R_P$$
Step 8Substitute into the resistance rule: $$R_T = 1.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega} + 5.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = 6.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$$

Step 1Sketch the circuit: Redraw the loop with $R_2$ replaced by a plain wire, add a clockwise current $I$, and mark $V_3$ across $R_3$ with $+$ where the current enters.
Step 2List the knowns: $V_S = 20\ \mathrm{V}$, $R_1 = 1.5\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, $R_3 = 3.9\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, and $R_2$ shorted so $R_2 = 0\ \Omega$. Asked for: $V_3$ with the fault present.
Step 3State the model: A short has effectively zero resistance and drops $0\ \mathrm{V}$, so only $R_1$ and $R_3$ remain in the path.
Step 4Set the convention: Current is clockwise; resistances stay in kilohms so the divider ratio is unitless.
Step 5Write the series resistance rule for the faulted circuit: $$R_T = R_1 + R_3$$
Step 6Substitute into the resistance rule: $$R_T = 1.5\ \mathrm{k\Omega} + 3.9\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = 5.4\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$$
Step 7Write the voltage divider rule for $R_3$: $$V_3 = \frac{R_3}{R_T}\, V_S$$
Step 8Substitute into the divider rule: $$V_3 = \frac{3.9\ \mathrm{k\Omega}}{5.4\ \mathrm{k\Omega}} \cdot 20\ \mathrm{V}$$
Step 9State the result: $V_3 = 14.4\ \mathrm{V}$.
Step 10Check the answer: Before the fault $V_3 = \dfrac{3.9}{8.1} \cdot 20\ \mathrm{V} = 9.63\ \mathrm{V}$, so the reading has risen, as expected when a short raises the current. With the short, $V_1 = 5.56\ \mathrm{V}$ and $5.56 + 0 + 14.4 = 20\ \mathrm{V}$.
